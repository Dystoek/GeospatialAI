# GeospatialAI
How can we estimate soil moisture and soil quality across large geographic regions where sensor measurements are sparse?

This question is central to environmental monitoring, sustainable agriculture, and climate modeling. To explore this problem, I recently developed a research-oriented geospatial machine learning pipeline focused on soil moisture estimation and soil quality mapping.

The project integrates geospatial soil composition data with machine learning and spatial modeling techniques to generate continuous soil moisture surfaces and identify regions with favorable soil conditions.

The workflow combines several analytical components:

• Development of a deep learning model using PyTorch to predict soil moisture from spatial coordinates, soil composition, and auxiliary environmental variables
• Construction of a Soil Quality Index to identify regions with balanced soil texture and favorable moisture conditions
• Application of Gaussian Process spatial interpolation to simulate continuous soil moisture surfaces from scattered observations
• Spatial error analysis to understand where predictive models perform well and where uncertainty remains
• Creation of 3D geospatial terrain visualizations to analyze spatial soil moisture patterns and environmental gradients

These approaches illustrate how data-driven models can complement traditional environmental monitoring by estimating soil conditions in regions where direct sensor observations are limited.

Such techniques are closely aligned with research directions in Earth observation, geospatial AI, and environmental monitoring, which are key focus areas at the German Aerospace Center.

I am particularly interested in exploring how machine learning, geospatial modeling, and remote sensing can contribute to climate monitoring, sustainable land management, and environmental intelligence.
